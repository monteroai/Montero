Status texts

Customers stop calling to ask "is my car done?" because the shop texts them first.

How to build it 5 STEPS

The texts it sends

CITY AUTO
Your Civic is checked in. Ready by 4:00 PM.
Front brakes are worn. $240 to replace. Tap to approve.
Approve
Your Civic is ready. Pay here: [link]
Thanks! Keys are in the lockbox by the door. Code 4821.

Work order

  1. 01Make the board: a shared sheet, one row per car, with a Status column (In, Working, Needs OK, Done).
  2. 02Connect a texting service with an automation tool. When a Status cell changes, send that status's text to the owner.
  3. 03For Needs OK, attach the photo and a reply link. An approval moves the car back to Working.
  4. 04For Done, include a payment link. Once it's paid, send the lockbox code.
  5. 05Get the customer's OK to text at check-in and include "Reply STOP to opt out".

Tools that can do it

Google SheetsZapier or MakeTwilioSquare or Stripe links

Many shop management programs already have texting and photo inspections built in. Check yours before building from scratch.

Pickup slots

A lunch rush that runs itself: everyone orders ahead and picks a time.

How to build it 5 STEPS

Work order

  1. 01Turn on online ordering with scheduled pickup times in your point-of-sale system.
  2. 02Make slots, for example every 15 minutes, and cap how many orders each one takes.
  3. 03Send each order to the kitchen a set time before its slot, not the moment it's placed.
  4. 04Text the customer when it's ready, with the shelf or locker number.
  5. 05Put the ordering link on the door, your Google listing and your bio.

Tools that can do it

Square OnlineToastCloverPickup shelf or lockers

Delivery apps take a cut of every order. Your own ordering page usually costs less on orders you'd have gotten anyway.
